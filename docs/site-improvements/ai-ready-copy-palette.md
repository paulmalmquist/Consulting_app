# AI-Ready Messaging — Copy Palette and Swap Plan

A rewrite proposal for novendor.ai. Goal: make the central marketing message about getting a company's data, process, and playbooks AI-ready, in language a busy CTO or CFO would actually nod at. Replace abstract "controlled execution layer" framing with human language that names the real problem.

Style guardrails: follow `docs/anti-ai-style.md`. No "harness," "leverage," "unlock," "robust," "seamless," "transformative," "in today's fast-paced world," em-dashes as confetti, or three-item lists with identical rhythm. Short sentences. Specific nouns. Verbs that mean something.

---

## 1. The pitch in one paragraph

> Most companies are not behind on AI. They are behind on the work that has to happen before AI is useful — clean data, written-down playbooks, and a process that runs the same way twice. We do that work. Then the AI you buy, build, or already pay for actually pays off.

Use this paragraph as the seed. Every variant below is a way of saying it for a different reader.

---

## 2. Headline variants — top of homepage

Pick one as the hero. Rotate the others into industry pages and CFO/CTO landing pages so the site stops repeating itself.

**Plain and direct**
- Get your data ready before your AI strategy depends on it.
- Your AI is only as good as the data underneath it. We fix the data underneath.
- The hard part of AI isn't the model. It's everything you have to clean up before the model sees it.
- AI-ready means your data, your playbooks, and your process can survive being automated.

**For the exec under pressure**
- Your board wants AI results this quarter. Your data isn't ready. Start here.
- You've been asked to deliver AI outcomes. You know your inputs are a mess. We start with the inputs.
- Stop running AI pilots on broken plumbing.

**Edge / point of view**
- AI doesn't fix bad process. It speeds it up.
- Most "failed AI projects" are actually failed data projects with an AI label.
- Buy less software. Write down more of how you actually work. Then add AI.

**For founders who like a hook**
- The new prerequisite for AI is boring: written playbooks, clean data, one source of truth.
- Before you hire an AI team, hire someone to clean the room.

Recommended primary: **"Your AI is only as good as the data underneath it. We fix the data underneath."** It's specific, concrete, and reframes the buyer's problem in one line.

Pair with whichever headline lands.

- We work with operators who are under pressure to ship AI and know their data, process, and playbooks aren't ready for it. We make them ready — in weeks, on one workflow at a time, without a rip-and-replace project.
- Get your operations to the point where the next AI tool you adopt — copilot, agent, model, vendor — actually works the first time. We rebuild the data pipes, write down the playbook, and put the process in a system that runs the same way twice.
- Three things have to be true before AI helps your business: the data has to be clean, the playbook has to be written down, and the process has to run the same way twice. We make those three things true, in that order.
- We are not an AI vendor. We are the team that gets you ready to use one. Or many. Or your own.

## 4. Proof bullets to replace the current "Typical Results"

Current bullets read like a generic benchmark slide. Replace with bullets that name the *kind* of progress executives can actually report up:

- A data layer your reporting, your AI tools, and your auditors all agree on.
- Playbooks that exist outside someone's head, in a form an agent or a new hire can follow.
- A workflow that runs the same way on a Tuesday in March as it did in December.
- Reporting cycles cut from days to hours because the inputs no longer need a human to assemble them.
- The first AI use case that actually saves time, with the receipts to prove it to the CFO.

## 5. CTOs vs CFOs — different angles, same product

The current `/cfo` page is fine but vanilla. There is no `/cto` page. Both readers should feel addressed. They have different fears.

### CTO angle

What keeps them up: AI projects that look like demos and never make it to production. Vendor demos that fall apart on real data. A team trying to build agents on top of pipelines that break weekly. A board asking "why aren't we shipping AI?" while the engineers know the data isn't there.

**Headline options**
- Your AI roadmap is blocked by your data, not your engineers.
- Stop shipping AI demos. Start shipping AI in production.
- Your model layer is fine. Your data layer isn't.
- The reason your last AI pilot stalled is the same reason your next one will.

**Lede**
> We work with CTOs who have the talent, the budget, and the mandate to ship AI — and who know their data, integrations, and process documentation aren't ready to support it. We close that gap. One workflow at a time, with your team, on your stack.

**Proof bullets**
- Source-system contracts so the next pipeline failure is visible the moment it happens, not three days later in a Monday standup.
- A semantic layer your AI tools can query without a translation layer for every new use case.
- Written-down process logic — the kind a coding agent can read and a junior engineer can follow.
- A pilot that runs in parallel with the current process until the outputs match. Then you cut over.

**CTA**: "Show me where the data layer is blocking the AI layer."

### CFO angle (rewrite of current `/cfo` page)

What keeps them up: AI spend going up, AI ROI not showing up, software stack getting wider, board asking what the company is doing about AI, audit committee asking whether the AI tools are touching anything they shouldn't.

**Headline**
- The AI line item on next year's budget needs an answer. Start with the data underneath it.
- Your AI spend is going up. Your AI ROI isn't. The data layer is why.
- Before you buy more AI software, fix the inputs. The ROI shows up faster.

**Lede**
> We work with CFOs who are being asked to fund an AI strategy and to defend the result. We start where AI actually pays back: the reporting cycle, the reconciliation work, the manual handoffs that swallow finance hours every month. We fix the data and the process first. The AI part comes after, and it works.

**Proof bullets**
- A reporting pack that comes from one source, not seven, with the math defensible to the auditor.
- A 25–40% drop in manual reconciliation work, freeing the team to do the analysis the business actually needs.
- An AI line item with a use case attached, not a category-level guess.
- A control trail for every AI-touched workflow, so the audit committee has nothing to flag.

**CTA**: "Find the three workflows where AI would pay back this year."

## 6. Vertical-specific variants

Each of these should replace the current "in 12 weeks with a controlled execution layer" tail on the four industry pages and the homepage industries section. The existing copy is pattern-matched and reads like the same paragraph four times.

### Real Estate Private Equity (CFO/COO)

- **Hero**: Make your fund data AI-ready before your LPs start asking what AI you use.
- **Sub**: Your IRRs, your waterfall, your investor reporting — all of it lives in spreadsheets that take a week to assemble. No AI tool will help until that changes. We fix the data layer that LP reporting, deal screening, and any future AI agent will all sit on top of.
- **Pain hook**: "If a model can't read your fund data without a human in the middle, neither can a GP."

### Consumer Credit (Chief Risk / Chief Credit)

- **Hero**: Get your credit data, your policy, and your exception logic into a form an AI can actually use — and an examiner can actually read.
- **Sub**: Underwriting AI is only as defensible as the policy it cites and the data it sees. We get your policy out of PDFs and into a system, your decision data out of inboxes and into a pipeline, and your overrides out of folklore and into an audit trail. Then the AI you deploy is something you can defend in a regulator meeting.
- **Pain hook**: "An AI that can't show its work isn't a credit decision. It's a liability."

### Medical / Revenue Cycle (CFO / VP Rev Cycle)

- **Hero**: AI won't fix your denial backlog if your prior auth data lives in seven payer portals.
- **Sub**: Every revenue cycle AI vendor pitches the same demo. None of them work until your authorization, denial, and payer reconciliation data live in one place with one set of rules. We do that part — the unglamorous part — so the AI you buy actually moves the cash conversion cycle.
- **Pain hook**: "The reason your AI pilot didn't reduce denials is the reason your team can't either: nobody can see the queue in one place."

### Legal Ops (COO / Director of Legal Operations)

- **Hero**: Your matter data, your billing narratives, your intake logic — written down, in one place, before you turn an AI loose on any of it.
- **Sub**: Legal AI vendors assume you have a clean matter taxonomy, consistent intake fields, and time entries that mean the same thing across the firm. You don't. We get you there, so the AI tools you pick — drafting, review, billing — actually work without three months of cleanup per matter type.
- **Pain hook**: "If two partners can't agree on what the intake form means, no AI is going to."

### Construction / PDS (CFO / VP PDS)

- **Hero**: Get your project data out of email and PDFs before you put AI on top of it.
- **Sub**: Change orders in inboxes. RFIs in PDF. Cost data in three systems that don't agree. We rebuild the project data layer that every AI tool you've evaluated assumed was already there. Then the AI does the work it's supposed to — flagging cost drift, surfacing schedule risk, drafting the executive update — without inventing numbers.
- **Pain hook**: "AI can't read a PDF email chain. Neither can your CFO at midnight on a Friday."

## 11. What to avoid in the rewrite

- "Unlock," "harness," "leverage" — all on the kill list.
- "AI-native" — it's everywhere already and means nothing to a CFO.
- "Transform" — kill it. "Get ready," "fix," "make it run" are stronger.
- Three-bullet lists where every bullet is the same length. Vary the rhythm.
- "It's not just X — it's Y" constructions. Banned.
- The pattern of ending every hero with "in 12 weeks with a controlled execution layer." Pick a tail per page. Some pages should have no tail.

---

## 12. Verification checklist

After the swaps land:

1. Open `https://novendor.ai` on desktop and mobile. The hero should say "AI-ready" or directly imply it within the first sentence.
2. Visit `/cfo`, `/industries/real-estate-private-equity`, `/industries/consumer-credit`, `/industries/medical`, `/industries/legal`, `/capabilities/comprehensive-data-strategy`. Each hero should sound different. None should end in the same boilerplate tail.
3. Read the page out loud. If any sentence sounds like a competitor's site, rewrite it.
4. Search for the words on the kill list (`harness`, `leverage`, `unlock`, `seamless`, `robust`, `holistic`, `transformative`, `streamline`, `empower`). Result count should be zero in the marketing folder.
5. Confirm `SloganBadge` either changes or is demoted from the dominant hero pixel — `Own Your Operating Logic` is fine as a secondary stamp, not the headline.
6. Have one CTO and one CFO read the homepage cold. The right reaction is "yeah, that's my problem." Anything else means the copy is still abstract.
