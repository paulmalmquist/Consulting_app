# AI Prompting 101 — Class Summary Notes

Captured verbatim from the wrap-up email body (Brittany Pfister, GC AI, 2026-05-05).

## Core Prompting Framework

- Treat AI like a smart, overeager intern who's read the entire internet.
- Context is king — be specific about needs and desired response format.
- System prompts vs user prompts:
  - System: standing orders (e.g. GC AI knows you're a lawyer).
  - User: ad-hoc instructions for specific tasks.
- AI is literal, non-deterministic, and wants to please.
- Each chat remembers conversation history, but not other chats.

## Anatomy of Great Prompts

- Tell the AI what you're giving it (e.g. "attached agreement with UCLA").
- State the desired action clearly ("draft an email").
- Specify format requirements (short paragraphs, major terms, final recommendation).
- Provide tone/audience context (e.g. "VP of Strategy and Development").
- Use "please" and "thank you" — produces better results.

## Interactive Exercise Results (from class)

- Document analysis / summary tasks: mostly 8–9/10 ratings.
- Drafting exercises: 8–9/10 across various use cases.
  - RFP drafting
  - Breach notice letters
  - FAQ creation from legislation
- Research prompts: 9/10 for complex multi-state surveys.

## Advanced Prompting Techniques

- Request emojis and tables for glanceable results.
- Ask for analysis / reasoning before the final answer.
- Give the AI a specific persona / role for context.
- Define terms of art (e.g. "option agreement" means different things in different practices).
- Use color coding (red / yellow / green) for risk assessments.

## Class Agenda — Four Core Legal AI Applications (from prework email)

1. **Distilling** — condensing complex legal documents into key points.
2. **Reviewing** — analyzing documents for compliance, risks, or specific criteria.
3. **Drafting** — creating legal documents from templates or requirements.
4. **Researching** — finding relevant cases, statutes, or legal principles.
